LeMMA: Learning Math by Meta-Adaptation

Zeyneb N. Kaya, Nick Rui, Sandra Yang · Stanford CS229 · pdf

Abstract. Automated theorem proving in Lean 4 is bottlenecked by data scarcity, and existing provers train on static synthetic datasets applied uniformly to every problem. LeMMA reframes theorem proving as a test-time meta-learning task: given a formal theorem statement, the model first generates related synthetic lemmas and proofs, a Weigher model scores them by their utility for the target problem, and the prover adapts its parameters on this weighted, problem-specific dataset before attempting a verifiable proof. The Weigher is trained with a bilevel-optimization RL objective (GRPO) using LoRA and mixed-mode bilevel differentiation for memory-efficient meta-gradients. On a 100-theorem subset of miniF2F, LeMMA improves over DeepSeek-Prover-V2 by 43%, suggesting that spending test-time compute on meta-adaptation yields better returns than scaling sample budgets.
